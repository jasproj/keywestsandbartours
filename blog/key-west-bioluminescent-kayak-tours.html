Bioluminescent Kayaking in Key West: What Really Glows, and the Night Tour to Book

Two different things glow at night in the Keys. One is bioluminescence: plankton that flash when the water moves, on warm, moonless nights, never on a schedule. The other is the nightly clear-kayak tour, where LED lights in the hull light up the flats beneath you. This page tells them apart, then shows you what to book.

What you're booking

The night clear-kayak tour, explained

LED-lit clear kayaks

Transparent hulls with lights built in. The glow under your seat comes from the kayak, and it lights the bottom, seagrass and whatever swims through.

What you see

Stingrays lifting off the sand, small nurse sharks, needlefish, crabs. Night is when the flats get busy, and the lit hull is your window.

When and how long

One departure nightly at 8 PM, about 90 minutes on the water. Guided, with a short paddling briefing first.

Who can go

Ages 4 and up. Kids ride in a double with an adult. No kayak experience needed; the route is shallow, protected water at an easy pace.

Where

Key West. Singles and doubles; the double is the practical pick for a parent paddling with a younger kid.

Bring

Clothes that can get wet, a dry layer for winter nights, and a dry bag for the phone. Phone cameras struggle in the dark; watch with your eyes.

The real thing

Natural bioluminescence in the Keys

What it is

Dinoflagellates, single-celled plankton, flash blue-green when the water around them is disturbed. A paddle stroke, a fish, your hand trailing over the side.

When

Warm water builds the population, so late spring through fall is the season. The week around the new moon is the window: a bright moon washes the glow out the way city lights wash out stars. Overcast nights help.

Where

Shallow backcountry flats and mangrove edges away from the lights of Duval. The darker the water, the more you see.

How bright

Honestly, variable. Some nights every stroke draws a streak; most nights it's a faint shimmer, and some nights nothing. No Keys operator sells it as a guaranteed show, and you should be wary of any that does.

Best odds

Book a night paddle in summer or early fall in the new-moon week, and ask the guide to kill the LEDs for a few minutes once your eyes adjust. You'll see whatever the water is doing that night.

Want a sure thing

Puerto Rico has three bioluminescent bays where the glow is dense enough to be the whole point of the trip. Our sister site covers Puerto Rico bio bay tours.

Tips

Get the most out of a night on dark water

Book the new-moon window

Darkest water. Check a moon calendar before you lock dates, whether you're hoping for natural glow or just want the LED show to pop.

No white flashlights

Your eyes need 20 minutes to adjust, and one phone light resets everyone. Red mode if you need light.

Trail your hand

Fingers in the water at paddling speed. If the plankton are up, this is how you'll know.

Dress for drips

Every stroke drips on you. Winter nights on the water run about 10 degrees cooler than the dock.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is there bioluminescent kayaking in Key West?

There is a nightly clear-kayak tour in Key West, but its glow comes from LED lights in the kayaks, not from bioluminescent plankton. Natural bioluminescence does occur in the Keys on warm, moonless nights, but no operator guarantees it.

How much does the Key West night kayak tour cost?

The Luminous Lagoon tour is $79.95 for a single clear kayak and $149.95 for a double. On Big Pine Key, Island Glow's Sunset Night Glow Tour is $75 per single rider and $130 for a tandem.

Can kids do the night kayak tour?

Yes. The Key West tour takes ages 4 and up, riding in a double with an adult. The Big Pine Key tour takes all ages with one paddler 16 or older per kayak.

When is the best time to see bioluminescence in the Florida Keys?

Warm months, late spring through fall, in the week around the new moon, on dark backcountry water away from town lights. Even then it varies night to night.

Where is the closest guaranteed bioluminescent bay?

Puerto Rico. Mosquito Bay on Vieques, Laguna Grande in Fajardo and La Parguera are dedicated bio bays where the glow is the whole point of the trip.
