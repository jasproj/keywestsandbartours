17 Best Things to Do in Islamorada

Updated October 2026 • 10 min read • Mile Marker 80–90, about 80 miles / 1 hr 45 min from Key West

Islamorada is a chain of six islands between Mile Marker 72 and 90, about 80 miles up the Overseas Highway from Key West and 90 minutes south of Miami. It calls itself the Sportfishing Capital of the World, and the marinas back that up, but the reason most people stop is the water on both sides of the road: Florida Bay and its mangrove islands to the west, Alligator Reef and the sandbar flats to the east.

This guide is built around what you can actually book and do in a day or two. Prices are what the operators list on FareHarbor; every Check Availability button opens the operator's own calendar. Start with the Islamorada tours page if you only want the boat trips.

On the water

1. Anchor at the Islamorada sandbar

The sandbar sits in Florida Bay a short run from the marinas: knee-deep water, a sand bottom and a crowd of boats on weekends. Shared trips run about 3 hours and cost $80–$130 per person; private charters start around $300 for the boat. Morning trips get glassier water, afternoons get the party. Bring water shoes, the bottom has shell.

2. Snorkel Alligator Reef Lighthouse

The 1873 iron lighthouse marks a reef 4 miles offshore with 10–20 ft of water, parrotfish, snapper and the occasional nurse shark. Shared catamaran trips from Tavernier run $60–$80 per adult with gear; two-hour private charters start at $300. Summer visibility is best; winter cold fronts can blow it out for a day.

3. Feed the tarpon at Robbie's Marina

Robbie's at Mile Marker 77.5 has been hand-feeding tarpon off its dock since 1976. Admission is a couple of dollars and a bucket of bait a few more; the fish are 60–150 lb and come up out of the water for it. Pelicans will try to steal the bait. Go early or late to avoid the mid-day crush, and walk the flea-market stalls while you are there.

4. Paddle the mangroves on a clear kayak

Islamorada's bayside mangrove creeks are flat, shallow and sheltered, which is why clear-bottom kayak tours work here. A guided 90-minute sunset paddle runs about $79 per person; morning eco-paddles through the mangroves and manatee channels from Tavernier are $64. No experience needed, and the clear hulls make the nurse sharks and rays under you the whole point.

6. Sunset cruise through the mangroves

Islamorada sunsets happen over Florida Bay, so the best seat is a boat on the bayside. Shared sunset cruises run $65–$90 per adult for about 90 minutes; private sunset charters for up to six start around $300–$380 for the boat. Lorelei's and Morada Bay are the bars to watch it from if you stay on land.

Fishing

7. Backcountry flats fishing

The bayside flats and Everglades edge hold bonefish, tarpon, redfish and snook. A half-day on a flats skiff with a guide runs $500–$700 for one or two anglers, full days $900–$1,000. Tarpon season is April through July; bonefish are year-round but best in spring and fall. Guides supply tackle and licenses are covered on the boat.

8. Offshore and reef charters

Sailfish, mahi, tuna and wahoo run off Islamorada in winter and spring; half-day reef and wreck trips for snapper and grouper run year-round. Private charters for up to six start around $450 for a half-day and $800–$2,000 for a full day offshore. Many local restaurants will cook your catch for a per-person fee.

Islands, parks and history

9. Indian Key Historic State Park

A 10-acre island a half-mile offshore that was the Dade County seat in 1836 until it was burned in the Second Seminole War. You reach it by kayak or boat only; the foundations and streets are still laid out and there is a short loop trail and a swimming spot. Rent a kayak at Robbie's (about 30 minutes each way) or join a guided paddle.

10. Lignumvitae Key Botanical State Park

Across the highway on the bayside, Lignumvitae Key is the highest point in the Keys at 16–18 ft and holds one of the last virgin tropical hardwood hammocks in the US. Access is by boat or kayak with ranger-led tours on a set schedule; check the park calendar before you paddle out. Mosquito repellent is not optional.

11. Windley Key Fossil Reef Geological State Park

A former quarry at Mile Marker 85.5 where the 1900s railroad cut blocks out of fossilized coral. The 8-ft quarry walls show star and brain coral in cross-section. Small entry fee, self-guided trails, about an hour.

12. History of Diving Museum

Mile Marker 83, bayside. Hard-hat helmets, rebreathers, treasure-salvage gear and the Keys' own diving history across a dozen rooms. Plan 60–90 minutes; it is the best rainy-day stop in the Upper Keys.

13. Theater of the Sea

A 1946 marine park at Mile Marker 84.5 with dolphin, sea lion and parrot shows plus swim-with programs. General admission runs under $50; dolphin swims are priced separately and sell out in season. Shows run on a loop, so two to three hours covers it.

14. Florida Keys Wild Bird Center

In Tavernier at Mile Marker 93.6, a rehab sanctuary with a boardwalk through the mangroves past pelicans, hawks, owls and herons that can't be released. Donation entry, guided sanctuary tours about $25, and the wild pelicans show up for the afternoon feeding.

Eat and drink

15. Islamorada Food Tour

A guided four-hour walk-and-ride through the town's fish houses and bars with tastings at each stop, about $119 per person. There is a golf-cart version and a happy-hour version for groups that would rather not walk. Good first-day orientation if you are staying a few nights.

16. Florida Keys Brewing Company and the Morada Way Arts District

Mile Marker 81.5 oceanside. The brewery's beer garden anchors a two-block strip of galleries and studios that hosts an Art Walk on the third Thursday of each month. Hogfish sandwiches at Islamorada Fish Company and Lazy Days are the two classic lunches within a mile.

17. Lorelei Restaurant and Cabana Bar

Bayside at Mile Marker 82, the sunset bar everyone ends up at. Live music nightly, a dock you can tie a boat to, and the sun goes down directly in front of the tables. Arrive 45 minutes before sunset in season for a seat.

Planning the drive?

Islamorada is a day trip from Key West only if you leave early: it is 1 hour 45 minutes each way with no traffic. Better as an overnight on the way in or out. Every trip here is booked direct with the operator.

FAQ

Islamorada Questions

How far is Islamorada from Key West?

About 80 miles and 1 hour 45 minutes on the Overseas Highway with no traffic. From Miami it is roughly 90 minutes. Most people stop in Islamorada on the drive in or out rather than as a day trip from Key West.

What is the best thing to do in Islamorada?

A sandbar trip on Florida Bay is the signature day: shared boats run $80–$130 per person for about 3 hours. Pair it with Robbie's tarpon feeding and a sunset at Lorelei and you have the town in one day.

Can you snorkel from shore in Islamorada?

Not well. The reef is 4 miles offshore at Alligator Reef, so you need a boat. Shared snorkel trips from Islamorada and Tavernier run $60–$80 per adult with gear included.

Is Islamorada good for kids?

Yes. Theater of the Sea, Robbie's tarpon, the glass-bottom boat ($75 adult) and the Wild Bird Center are all easy with children, and the sandbar is knee-deep. Beaches are small; Anne's Beach at Mile Marker 73 is the one shallow swimming beach.
