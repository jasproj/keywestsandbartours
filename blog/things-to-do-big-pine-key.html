16 Best Things to Do in Big Pine Key

Updated October 2026 • 10 min read • Mile Marker 29–33, about 30 miles / 45 minutes from Key West

Big Pine Key is 30 miles and 45 minutes up the road from Key West, the biggest island in the Lower Keys and the quietest. There is no downtown. What there is: a herd of knee-high Key deer that wander the streets, the best reef in the Lower Keys 5 miles offshore at Looe Key, Bahia Honda State Park next door, and backcountry water in every direction.

This is the easiest day trip from Key West and most of it is free. The boat trips below are booked direct with the operators; the Lower Keys tours page has every charter from Big Pine, Cudjoe, Summerland and Sugarloaf.

Key deer and the refuge

1. See the Key deer

Key deer are a subspecies of white-tailed deer the size of a large dog, found nowhere but these islands; about 1,000 remain. The National Key Deer Refuge covers most of Big Pine and No Name Key. You will see them at dawn and dusk on Key Deer Boulevard and the side streets, and almost always on No Name Key. Do not feed them, it is a federal offense and the reason they get hit by cars. The speed limit drops to 35 at night for this reason.

2. Blue Hole

An old quarry on Key Deer Boulevard, now a freshwater pond with an observation platform, alligators, turtles and the deer that come to drink. Free, five minutes from the highway, a 10-minute stop. The Watson and Mannillo nature trails start nearby for a short walk through pine rockland.

3. No Name Pub

At the end of Watson Boulevard on the way to No Name Key, a 1936 bar with roughly $100,000 in signed dollar bills stapled to every surface. Pizza and a beer; cash still matters here. Combine it with the drive to No Name Key for deer at dusk.

On the water

4. Snorkel Looe Key

Looe Key is a spur-and-groove reef 5 miles south of Big Pine in 5–35 ft of water inside a sanctuary preservation area: no fishing, no lobstering, which is why the fish are big and tame. Shared half-day boats from Ramrod Key run about $60 per snorkeler with gear, three sites, four hours. It is the best shallow reef between Key West and Marathon.

9. Fish the backcountry or the reef

Lower Keys guides work the Gulf-side flats for tarpon, permit and bonefish and the reef for snapper and grouper. Two-hour charters start around $400 for the boat, half-days $650–$800, lobster trips in season from $650. The Content Keys and the Marquesas are full-day runs.

Beaches and parks

10. Bahia Honda State Park

Mile Marker 37, 8 miles north of Big Pine. Calusa Beach on the bay side and Sandspur on the ocean side are the best natural beaches in the Keys, with a kayak concession, a campground that books 11 months out, and the ruined 1912 railroad bridge you can walk up for the view. Entry about $8 per vehicle; the gate closes when the lots fill, usually by 10–11am in winter.

11. Veterans Memorial Park and Little Duck Key

At the Marathon end of the Seven Mile Bridge, a free county beach with picnic tables, shade and calm water. Smaller and quieter than Bahia Honda and never closes for capacity.

12. Long Beach on Big Pine

A rough, natural shoreline at the south end of Long Beach Drive: seaweed, shallow flats and almost nobody. Not a swimming beach, but the best place on the island to watch the sun go down over the water for free.

Land

13. Grimal Grove tropical fruit tour

A restored 1950s fruit grove on Big Pine with mango, sapodilla, jackfruit, breadfruit and 90 other tropical species, run as a nonprofit. The 90-minute guided tasting tour is $38 and changes with what is ripe. Saturdays are market days.

14. Big Pine Flea Market

Weekends at Mile Marker 30, oceanside: fishing tackle, produce, t-shirts and whatever fell off a boat. Open from 8am, done by early afternoon, cash.

15. Lunch at Little Palm Island

The private-island resort off Little Torch Key opens its restaurant to non-guests for lunch and dinner by reservation. The launch leaves from the Little Torch dock; expect $100 or more per person before drinks, and Key deer on the island grounds.

16. Bike the Lower Keys

The Overseas Heritage Trail runs through Big Pine along US-1, and the back roads to No Name Key are flat, empty and lined with deer. Rentals in Big Pine and Key West; bring water, there is little shade.

FAQ

Big Pine Key Questions

Where can you see Key deer?

On Big Pine Key and No Name Key, mostly at dawn and dusk along Key Deer Boulevard, around the Blue Hole and on the roads to No Name Key. They are protected; feeding them is illegal and the main cause of road deaths.

How far is Big Pine Key from Key West?

About 30 miles and 45 minutes. It is the closest Lower Keys island to Key West with a reef boat, a state park beach next door and the deer, so it is the standard half-day or full-day trip out of town.

Is Looe Key better than snorkeling from Key West?

Looe Key is a protected reef in 5–35 ft of water with more and bigger fish than most Key West sites, and the boat ride is shorter. Shared trips run about $60 per snorkeler for four hours from Ramrod Key.

Does Bahia Honda fill up?

Yes, in winter and on weekends the park closes its gate when the parking lots fill, often by mid-morning. Arrive before 10am or go after 2pm when the morning crowd leaves.
