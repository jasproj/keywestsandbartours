Iguanas in Key West: Where to See Them, Why They're Everywhere & the One Iguana Tour

You'll spot them on seawalls, in the palms and all over the walls of Fort Zach: green iguanas, some bright green, the big males burnt orange. They're an invasive species, and there's even a boat tour that hunts them.

Short answer: Yes, Key West has lots of iguanas. They're non-native green iguanas, easy to spot anywhere there's sun, water and trees, and best seen mid-morning to early afternoon while they bask. Spotting

Where to see iguanas in Key West

You don't have to look hard. For the most, go mid-morning to early afternoon on a warm day, when they're out basking.

Fort Zachary Taylor

The rocky shoreline, the moat and the brick walls of the fort. Iguanas bask on the stone and climb the walls.

Key West Tropical Forest & Botanical Garden

On Stock Island. Iguanas lounge in the trees and along the paths; several photos on this page were taken here.

Higgs Beach & West Martello

Seawalls and the garden grounds at West Martello Tower draw basking iguanas.

Key West Cemetery

Old trees and quiet grounds in the middle of Old Town.

Smathers Beach

Check the vegetation behind the beach.

Any seawall or dock

Iguanas sun themselves on concrete and rock near water all over the island.

The species

Why there are so many iguanas in Key West

Green iguanas (Iguana iguana) aren't native to Florida. Pets released or escaped into the wild found a warm climate, plenty of food and few predators, and the population took off.

Where they're from

Central America to tropical South America and some eastern Caribbean islands. Florida's population started with released and escaped pets.

How big

Males top five feet and can weigh up to 17 pounds. Females can also reach five feet but rarely pass seven pounds.

How many eggs

A female lays one clutch a year of 14 to 76 eggs, in a burrow.

What they eat

Mostly plants: leaves, flowers and fruit, including landscaping and native plants.

Swimmers

They swim well and often drop from a branch into the water when startled.

Cold snaps

They aren't cold hardy. In a hard cold snap they go limp and can fall out of trees: Florida's “frozen iguanas.”

The problem

Why Florida wants them gone

Seawalls and sidewalks

Their burrows erode and collapse sidewalks, foundations, seawalls, berms and canal banks.

Landscaping

They strip flowering plants and gardens.

Native plants

They eat plants that endangered species depend on for habitat.

Droppings

Iguana droppings can carry salmonella and foul docks, pools and patios.

The law

Florida iguana hunting rules

Rules checked against the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC) on September 29, 2026.

Prohibited species

Green iguanas have been a prohibited species in Florida since April 29, 2021. Keeping one as a pet now takes a permit, and they can't be sold as pets.

Private property

With the landowner's permission, iguanas can be removed and humanely killed year-round. No license or permit is needed.

Public land

Removal is allowed without a license or permit on 32 FWC-managed properties under an FWC executive order. Other public land needs authorization.

No relocating

Captured iguanas can't be released somewhere else.

Humane only

They're protected by Florida's anti-cruelty law, so any killing has to be humane.

Firearms

Discharging a firearm is prohibited in most of Key West. Check local ordinances before trying any removal yourself.

On the menu

Can you eat iguana?

Yes. Iguana is eaten across Central and South America, where it's called gallina de palo, chicken of the tree. It's lean and high in protein. Because iguanas can carry salmonella, the meat has to be cooked thoroughly.

Visiting

Iguana-watching etiquette

  • Keep your distance. They bite and whip their tails when cornered.
  • Don't feed them. Feeding keeps them near people and adds to the problem.
  • Watch your step. Droppings can carry salmonella.
  • Leash the dog. Dogs that chase iguanas can get bitten or scratched.
  • Bring a zoom lens. They sit still for long stretches while basking, which makes for easy photos.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Are there iguanas in Key West?

Yes. Green iguanas live all over Key West: on seawalls, in trees, at Fort Zachary Taylor, the cemetery and the botanical garden. You'll almost certainly see several on any visit.

Are iguanas in Key West dangerous?

Not usually. They're plant eaters and run from people. If cornered they can bite and whip their tails, and their droppings can carry salmonella, so keep your distance and don't feed them.

Is there an iguana tour in Key West?

Yes. Key West Surfari runs a private Guided Iguana Hunt EcoTour from Stock Island for up to six people: $875 for four hours or $1,200 for seven, per boat. In Miami, Salty Fresh Fishing Charters runs private iguana hunts from $600 for four hours.

Can you hunt iguanas in Key West?

On private property with the owner's permission, iguanas can be humanely removed year-round without a license. Public land needs authorization, releasing captured iguanas elsewhere is illegal, and firearms are banned in most of Key West.

Why are there so many iguanas in Key West?

Released and escaped pets established a population, the climate suits them, they have few predators, and each female lays 14 to 76 eggs a year.

Can you eat iguana?

Yes. Iguana is eaten across Central and South America, where it's called gallina de palo, or chicken of the tree. It has to be cooked thoroughly because iguanas can carry salmonella.
