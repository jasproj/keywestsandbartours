FAQ

Key West Snorkeling Questions

Where do Key West snorkeling tours go?

Most go to the reef line six to seven miles south of the island — Sand Key, Eastern Dry Rocks, Rock Key and Western Sambo, depending on wind and visibility that day. The captain picks the site the morning of. It is the only living coral barrier reef in the continental United States.

Is snorkeling in Key West good for beginners?

Yes. The reef sits in 5 to 25 feet of water, the shared boats supply mask, snorkel, fins and a flotation vest, and crew are in the water with you. Morning trips are calmer than afternoon ones; if anyone is nervous, book the morning.

Shared boat or private charter?

Shared trips run $39 to $129 per person and are the right call for one to four people. A private boat starts at $447 for up to six — under $75 a head — and you skip the crowd, pick your pace, and can add a sandbar or dolphin stop. From five people the private boat is usually cheaper.

What is included in the price?

Gear (mask, snorkel, fins, vest) on every shared trip and most private ones; drinks after the snorkel on most shared boats; lunch on a few of the combos. Not included: the 15–20% crew tip, and reef-safe sunscreen — bring your own, and make sure it is reef-safe.

What is the best time of day and year to snorkel?

Mornings, for calmer water and better visibility. May through September has the warmest, clearest water; winter fronts stir it up and cancel some trips. Weather cancellations by the captain are refunded.
